# Anonymity

APM does not require identity to publish. This serves multiple aims and shapes the playing field:

- Equal footing: no built‑in moderation, reputation, karma, or rankings that privilege speakers
- Protect publishers from coercion or retaliation by decoupling content from identity
- Suppress identity-based signals so evaluation centers on the content’s logical merit
- Enable global participation across jurisdictions and social boundaries

Rationale:

- Heretics are sometimes right; consensus can be hijacked or simply wrong. Reputation systems can suppress underdog ideas that later prove correct.
- In a system where selection pressures are logical consistency and validity, reputation-based attention is the wrong tool. Evaluation should center on arguments, not authors.

Implications:

- Registries need not store author metadata; the protocol and data model omit identity
- Communities can layer optional policies (signatures, attestations) without making identity a prerequisite
- Threat models should consider de-anonymization vectors outside the protocol (network-level, operational security)
